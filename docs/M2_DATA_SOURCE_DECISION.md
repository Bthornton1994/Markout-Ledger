# Milestone 2 data-source decision

Date checked for every source below: **2026-09-22** (the first version of this document, dated 2026-09-18, selected Bybit; that selection is withdrawn, §1). How sources were checked is stated per item, because this evaluation was done from an environment whose egress policy blocks every venue website: official documentation and code that venues publish in public GitHub repositories was read at a pinned commit (**[R]**); everything else (documentation pages, terms, pricing, historical archives, geographic restrictions) is known only through search-tool excerpts of the official page (**[S]**), which are not authoritative and must be re-read on the live page by the owner before capture begins; third-party implementations are corroboration only (**[T]**); anything that could not be verified is **[U]**, and nothing marked [U] or [S] is treated as a right, a guarantee or a permission. An independent citation check on 2026-09-22 could not re-run any search (the session's search budget was exhausted) and found that several [S] excerpts read as the search tool's summaries rather than page text; every [S] item below is therefore a pointer to a page a person must read, and where an [S] item is the only evidence for a rule, the contract relies on official code instead (§2 says which).

## 1. Decision

**Selected: Kraken spot `BTC/USD`, captured first party from the public WebSocket API v2 (`wss://ws.kraken.com/v2`): the `book` channel at depth 100 with its in-band snapshot and per-message CRC32 checksum, the `trade` channel, and the `instrument` channel.** Adapter mapping: M2_DATA_CONTRACT.md §8.

Why this one:

- The venue serves clients in the United States, where this project is operated; the official US quick-start page lists only New York and Maine as excluded states [S]. Bybit, the previous selection, excludes the United States on its official restricted-countries page (`bybit.com/en/help-center/article/Service-Restricted-Countries`), so its capture requirement was not executable; Binance's Terms of Use name the United States among its Restricted Locations and access from the US is reported blocked (HTTP 451) [S `binance.com/en/terms`].
- Public market data needs no account or key: the official Go SDK subscribes to `book`, `trade` and `instrument` with no token and calls `/0/public/*` without authentication [R]; the venue's WebSocket FAQ and introduction say the same [S].
- The book can be reconstructed **fail closed**: every book message carries a CRC32 checksum of the top 10 levels per side after the message is applied; the algorithm is implemented in the venue's official Go SDK and pinned with the venue's documented worked example in its official Rust SDK tests [R]. No sequence number exists on book messages (none in any official type or fixture [R]; none located in documentation [S]), and none is assumed.
- Trades name the taker side (`side`), carry a per-book sequential `trade_id`, the taker order type and an RFC 3339 timestamp with microseconds [R types, S descriptions]; several trades may share one message and batching does not imply one taker order [S].
- The instrument channel publishes explicit `price_increment`, `qty_increment`, `price_precision` and `qty_precision` per pair [R types, S example], so the precision check of contract §5.5 is mechanical.
- Every message shape, the checksum, the reconnection behaviour and the keepalive cadence were read from the venue's official repositories at pinned commits [R] (§5), so the adapter can be implemented without access to the website; the remaining semantics are [S] and listed as owner reading (§4).

Conditions attached to the decision (each is blocking until met; none is a checklist nicety):

- **C1, precision.** `BTC/USD` has `qty_precision` 8 (`qty_increment` `1e-08`) [S example, R fixture]; the engine's `QTY_SCALE` is `1e-6`. The normalizer refuses the instrument on the current engine (contract §5.5, R5). Either the versioned precision migration lands first (M2_GROK_HANDOFF.md §0, PR-0) or the owner substitutes a Kraken pair with `qty_precision <= 6` and `price_precision <= 6` after the P0 listing; no such pair has been verified from here (§4). Nothing is rounded or truncated in any case.
- **C2, rights and terms, read by a person.** US clients contract with Payward Interactive, Inc. under the Global Terms of Service [S]. The search-visible Global Terms say Kraken owns all content on its services and platforms ("Our Content"), permit use "only for their own benefit" under a revocable permission, prohibit copying, modifying, distributing or otherwise commercially exploiting Our Content, route any other use to `legal@kraken.com`, and prohibit "web scraping, web harvesting, or data extraction methods" and "bots, robots, parsers, spiders, scripts, programs, routines, or any other forms of automation" against the platform, in a sentence the search tool truncated [S]. Whether the documented public API is carved out of that automation clause is **unknown**; the venue publishes SDKs and a CLI for exactly that use, and its official SDK disclaimer says API use "remains governed by the applicable Kraken terms of service and API terms" [R], but no separate API terms page was found [U]. Therefore: before any capture, the owner reads the Global Terms in full (URLs in §4); if the automation clause does not plainly exempt use of the documented public API, the owner obtains written permission from `legal@kraken.com` first. No clause about AI or machine-learning training was located, which is an unknown, not a permission [U]; this matters because the owner's later milestones (a learned controller trained on replays, RLVR) are outside this milestone but are the stated direction of the project (M2_GROK_HANDOFF.md §2 excludes them from the build, not from the roadmap), so until the reading establishes otherwise no model is trained on any Kraken capture. Until the reading establishes more, the only admissible `rights` block for this venue is `redistribution: "unclear"` (or `"prohibited"`) with `publication: "hash_only"`; raw captures never enter Git; the repository carries hashes, provenance and schemas only (contract §6.3).
- **C3, jurisdiction.** The capture host and its operator are in a US state the venue serves (the excerpt names New York and Maine as excluded; whether the list is complete, and Washington's status in particular, is unknown [U]); no geographic workaround of any kind.
- **C4, fees.** The recorded scenario's `makerFeeBps` is read by a person from the fee page on the capture day and recorded with the run: the search-visible headline is 0.25% maker and 0.40% taker at the lowest tier with a tier-basis change on 2026-07-09 [S], while the venue's official CLI hard-codes a 0.26% taker "Starter tier default" (`README.md:778`) and a 0.16% maker figure (`skills/kraken-fee-optimization/SKILL.md:35`) [R], which conflicts and looks stale. The sensitivity grid sweeps the fee in any case.
- **C5, owner pre-capture checklist.** §4 lists every [S] and [U] item with its URL; each is read on the live page and its date recorded before the first capture.

**Alternates.** None is specified on the same contract. Coinbase (market-data terms) and Bitstamp (data mechanics) were evaluated (§3) and rejected; Gemini and Binance.US were evaluated and not selected, and are the next candidates if Kraken's terms reading (C2) fails (§3a); Bybit and Binance are rejected on jurisdiction; Hyperliquid is rejected on data mechanics and jurisdiction; Kuru `MON-USDC` on Monad remains a deferred research target for the reasons recorded on 2026-09-18 (block timestamps at one-second granularity with 300 ms blocks, AMM liquidity outside the order events, contract migration); third-party recorded data (Tardis.dev) is not a replay basis this build accepts.

### 3a. Gemini, Bitstamp and Binance.US

The three US-accessible alternatives were evaluated on the same checklist by the research pass of 2026-09-22 (rows above). Gemini and Binance.US publish official documentation or SDKs on GitHub, so their feed mechanics are [R]; both synchronize a book with `U`/`u` update ids against a snapshot and publish no checksum, both quote BTC quantities at `1e-8` (the same migration need as Kraken), and neither's US state availability, terms or fee schedule could be read (their availability, terms and fee pages could not be read from the authoring environment). Bitstamp has no official GitHub organisation; everything known about it is third party, and what is known (no in-band snapshot, no sequence number or checksum, crossed books documented as expected) rejects it on data mechanics. None was selected. If the owner's reading of Kraken's terms (C2) fails, Gemini and Binance.US are the two candidates to evaluate next, on a Binance-style adapter with the buffered-snapshot fold rule that this contract deliberately does not carry (contract §5.3, last bullet).
