## 1. Decision

**Selected provisionally: Kraken spot `BTC/USD`, captured first party from the public WebSocket API v2 (`wss://ws.kraken.com/v2`): the `book` channel at depth 100 with its in-band snapshot and per-message CRC32 checksum, the `trade` channel, and the `instrument` channel.** Adapter mapping: M2_DATA_CONTRACT.md §8. The selection holds only while conditions C1 to C5 below can be met; nothing is captured, and the P0 listing does not run, until C2 and C3 are met.

Why this one:

- The venue serves clients in the United States, where this project is operated; the official US quick-start page (`https://support.kraken.com/articles/quick-start-for-clients-in-the-united-states`) names New York and Maine as the states whose residents are not served, per search excerpts of 2026-09-22 and 2026-09-23 [S] (C3). Bybit, the previous selection, excludes the United States on its official restricted-countries page (`bybit.com/en/help-center/article/Service-Restricted-Countries`), so its capture requirement was not executable; Binance's Terms of Use name the United States among its Restricted Locations and access from the US is reported blocked (HTTP 451) [S `binance.com/en/terms`].
- Public market data needs no account or key: the official Go SDK subscribes to `book`, `trade` and `instrument` with no token and calls `/0/public/*` without authentication [R]; the venue's WebSocket FAQ and introduction say the same [S].
- The book can be reconstructed **fail closed**: every book message carries a CRC32 checksum of the top 10 levels per side after the message is applied; the algorithm is implemented in the venue's official Go SDK and pinned with the venue's documented worked example in its official Rust SDK tests [R]. No sequence number exists on book messages (none in any official type or fixture [R]; none located in documentation [S]), and none is assumed.
- Trades name the taker side (`side`), carry a per-book sequential `trade_id`, the taker order type and an RFC 3339 timestamp with microseconds [R types, S descriptions]; several trades may share one message and batching does not imply one taker order [S].
- The instrument channel publishes explicit `price_increment`, `qty_increment`, `price_precision` and `qty_precision` per pair [R types, S example], so the precision check of contract §5.5 is mechanical.
- Every message shape, the checksum, the reconnection behaviour and the keepalive cadence were read from the venue's official repositories at pinned commits [R] (§5), so the adapter can be implemented without access to the website; the remaining semantics are [S] and listed as owner reading (§4).

Conditions attached to the decision (each is blocking until met; none is a checklist nicety):

- **C1, precision.** `BTC/USD` has `qty_precision` 8 (`qty_increment` `1e-08`) [S example, R fixture]; the engine's `QTY_SCALE` is `1e-6`. The normalizer refuses the instrument on the current engine (contract §5.5, R5). Either the versioned precision migration lands first (M2_GROK_HANDOFF.md §0, PR-0) or the owner substitutes a Kraken pair with `qty_precision <= 6` and `price_precision <= 6` after the P0 listing; no such pair has been verified from here (§4). The choice between PR-0 and a substitute is not made before the P0 listing exists, and the listing itself waits for C2 and C3 (handoff §0). Nothing is rounded or truncated in any case.
- **C2, rights and terms (blocking, unresolved).** Source: the Global Terms of Service, `https://www.kraken.com/legal/global-terms` (US clients contract with Payward Interactive, Inc. [S]). What is known of the page as of 2026-09-23: the owner read the live page, reported broad restrictions on data extraction and automation, and found no explicit exception for the public WebSocket API [O]. Search excerpts are consistent with that. On 2026-09-22, excerpts attributed to the Global Terms said that Kraken owns the content on its services ("Our Content"), permits use "only for their own benefit" under a revocable permission, prohibits copying, distributing or otherwise commercially exploiting it, routes any other use to `legal@kraken.com`, and prohibits "web scraping, web harvesting, or data extraction methods" and "bots, robots, parsers, spiders, scripts, programs, routines, or any other forms of automation" in a sentence the search tool truncated [S]. On 2026-09-23, from a result set that held the Global, EEA and Custody Terms (one summary attributed the clause to the EEA Terms), excerpts described the same extraction and automation prohibitions and a prohibition on developing third-party applications that interact with the content without prior written consent [S]. The venue's API documentation, in a search excerpt of 2026-09-23 that the search tool attributes to the Exchange overview page (`https://docs.kraken.com/api/docs/guides/global-intro/`), requires Kraken's prior permission for "any non-personal commercial use of data from publicly accessible endpoints, such as market data, exchange status" [S]; that sentence adds a condition, it grants nothing for any other use, and whether this project's use is personal or commercial is not decided here [U]. The official Rust SDK's disclaimer says API use "remains governed by the applicable Kraken terms of service and API terms" [R `kraken-api-sdk` `DISCLAIMER.md:90-91`]; no separate API terms page was located [U]; no clause on AI or machine learning was located [U]. **Whether the extraction and automation restrictions apply to this project's use of the public API is unresolved.** This repository therefore claims no permission to capture, retain, redistribute, or train any model on Kraken market data, and none may be inferred from the venue publishing SDKs, from the public API needing no key, or from the absence of a clause.

  C2 is met only when the owner records, as a comment on this contract's pull request #3 (`https://github.com/Bthornton1994/Markout-Ledger/pull/3`) with its date, source and scope, either written clarification or permission from Kraken (the terms' route is `legal@kraken.com`) or a written review by a qualified lawyer acting for the owner (a review by the authors, the implementer or any AI tool does not count), **which concludes that each of the following is permitted**: automated first-party access to the public WebSocket API v2 and to the public REST endpoints the capture and P0 use (`AssetPairs`, `Time`); private retention of the raw captures on the owner's host; the research use of this project; and publication, in this public repository and its pull requests, of hashes, normalize reports and the derived values that P0 and A12 report. A record that is silent on any of these, or concludes against it, does not meet C2. Model training on any capture stays excluded unless the record expressly permits it (the owner's later milestones, a learned controller trained on replays and RLVR, are outside this build but are the project's stated direction, handoff §2).

  Gates that follow, each independent of the others: (a) until C2 and C3 are met, nothing is captured and the P0 listing does not run; (b) until the record that meets C2 also grants redistribution in writing, the only admissible `rights` block for this venue is `redistribution: "unclear"` (or `"prohibited"`) with `publication: "hash_only"`, and a block with `redistribution: "permitted"` names that record in its `note` (contract §6.3); (c) in every case raw captures never enter Git, and the repository carries hashes, provenance, schemas and constructed examples only (contract §6.3); (d) the owner runs every capture on the host confirmed under C3, and the implementer never runs a live capture and never receives raw captures or venue payloads (handoff A12).
- **C3, jurisdiction (blocking).** Source: the official US quick-start page, `https://support.kraken.com/articles/quick-start-for-clients-in-the-united-states`, checked on 2026-09-23 through search excerpts only (direct access refused, see the revision note): "Kraken does not offer services to residents of New York (NY), and Maine (ME)", with state-level restrictions on fiat transfers (IN, LA, MA, UT) and on EUR (TX, NH) [S]. Whether that list is complete is unknown [U], and the 2026-09-22 excerpts left Washington's spot status open [U]. C3 is met only when the owner confirms in writing on pull request #3, from the live page and with the date of that reading, that the capture host is located in, and its operator resides in, a US state the venue serves for spot (the state need not be named publicly). No geographic workaround of any kind: no VPN, proxy, remote host or third party in another jurisdiction.
- **C4, fees.** Source: the fee schedule, `https://www.kraken.com/features/fee-schedule`. The owner read its Spot Crypto table and reported Tier 1 at **0.40% maker and 0.80% taker** to the authors on 2026-09-23 [O]. The authors could not open the page (revision note); search summaries of 2026-09-23 give an overall spot range of -0.02% maker to 0.80% taker [S], consistent with that reading, while one summary repeated the figures this document carried before 2026-09-23, which the owner's reading supersedes and which are no longer used. The official CLI's hard-coded paper-trading fee [R `kraken-cli` `README.md:778`] is a tool default, not a fee source for this project. Since 2026-07-09 an account's tier is the best of its 30-day spot volume, futures volume and assets on platform, assessed point in time [S `https://support.kraken.com/articles/cross-platform-fee-tier-changes`]; designated lower-liquidity pairs have a separate maker-rebate schedule, and stablecoin and FX pairs have their own [S `https://support.kraken.com/articles/pairs-eligible-for-maker-fee-rebates`, `https://support.kraken.com/hc/en-us/articles/360039299431-How-do-stablecoin-fees-work-`]; whether any pair-specific rate applies to `BTC/USD` is unknown [U]. The REST `AssetPairs` response, which the capture records verbatim in its manifest (contract §8.1), carries a per-pair `fees` tier array and, when Kraken includes it, a `fees_maker` array (the official Rust SDK decodes `fees_maker` as optional, "empty when Kraken omits the field") [R `api-go` `pkg/spot/entities.go:226-227`, `kraken-api-sdk` `rust/src/api/market/types.rs:433-435, 548-549`]; whether the response to the capture's request carries it, and whether the arrays reflect the 2026-07-09 tier basis, is unknown [U]. None of this fixes the fee of a future capture: the schedule can change, the tier is an assumption about an account that does not exist, and a pair-specific rate can apply. Rule: on the capture day a person re-reads the fee schedule and the pair-specific pages and records with the run the URL, the date, the assumed tier (Tier 1, the entry tier, unless the owner documents another), the maker rate that applies to `BTC/USD` at that tier, whether a pair-specific schedule applies, and whether the manifest's `fees_maker`, if present, agrees (a derived value, never the payload). The engine takes a non-negative whole number of basis points (`feeOn` in `src/core/money.ts` rejects a negative rate; rebates are not modelled, ACCOUNTING.md): a rate that is not one is entered as the smallest non-negative whole number of basis points not below it (a rebate is entered as 0); the rate as read and the entered value are both recorded. The sensitivity grid also runs 0 (protocol §2).
- **C5, owner pre-capture checklist.** §4 lists every [S], [O] and [U] item with its URL; each is read on the live page and its date recorded before the first capture.

**Alternates.** None is specified on the same contract. Coinbase (market-data terms) and Bitstamp (data mechanics) were evaluated (§3) and rejected; Gemini and Binance.US were evaluated and not selected, and are the next candidates if C2 cannot be met (§3a); Bybit and Binance are rejected on jurisdiction; Hyperliquid is rejected on data mechanics and jurisdiction; Kuru `MON-USDC` on Monad remains a deferred research target for the reasons recorded on 2026-09-18 (block timestamps at one-second granularity with 300 ms blocks, AMM liquidity outside the order events, contract migration); third-party recorded data (Tardis.dev) is not a replay basis this build accepts.

### 3a. Gemini, Bitstamp and Binance.US

The three US-accessible alternatives were evaluated on the same checklist by the research pass of 2026-09-22 (rows above). Gemini and Binance.US publish official documentation or SDKs on GitHub, so their feed mechanics are [R]; both synchronize a book with `U`/`u` update ids against a snapshot and publish no checksum, both quote BTC quantities at `1e-8` (the same migration need as Kraken), and neither's US state availability, terms or fee schedule could be read (their availability, terms and fee pages could not be read from the authoring environment). Bitstamp has no official GitHub organisation; everything known about it is third party, and what is known (no in-band snapshot, no sequence number or checksum, crossed books documented as expected) rejects it on data mechanics. None was selected. If C2 cannot be met, Gemini and Binance.US are the two candidates to evaluate next, on a Binance-style adapter with the buffered-snapshot fold rule that this contract deliberately does not carry (contract §5.3, last bullet).
